Research

My research has moved from how structures fail to how construction itself can change.

The subjects have changed over time, but the underlying question is practical: how can we understand, strengthen, build and inspect structures more effectively?

Earlier work

Masonry and seismic behaviour.

My doctoral and early postdoctoral work focused on masonry infills in reinforced-concrete frames, cyclic loading and strengthening.

That work led to studies of skin reinforcement, latex-modified mortar and analytical modelling. It also resulted in an Indian patent for GI nettings for a partially restrained RCC beam–column joint.

More recent work

Printing, digital construction and AI.

The research now includes 3D concrete printing, printable cementitious materials, BIM, scan-to-BIM and machine learning.

Recent publications also explore computer vision and deep learning for crack characterisation. The direction is toward construction systems that can be designed, fabricated and monitored with greater precision.

Structural behaviour

Masonry infills, reinforced-concrete frames, cyclic loading and structural response.

Seismic strengthening

Skin reinforcement, confinement and materials for improving masonry performance.

3D concrete printing

Printable cementitious materials, extrusion, reinforcement and buildability.

Digital construction

BIM, scan-to-BIM and digital representations of the built environment.

Structural monitoring

Computer vision and deep learning for inspection and damage characterisation.

Construction automation

Multi-arm printing and the integration of reinforcement into automated construction.

Current project

TriFusion: multi-arm 3D printing with reinforcement.

The 2026 KSRG seed-funded project aims to develop a multi-arm 3D printing system that can integrate horizontal and vertical reinforcement, moving the research from printable material toward a construction system.
