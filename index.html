One Journey. Many Dimensions.

The classroom, the laboratory and the institution are the same job.

My work has taken me across the classroom, the laboratory, the research community and institutional leadership. Each role has reinforced the same conviction: knowledge becomes more meaningful when it is used to develop people, strengthen institutions and serve society.

I teach structural engineering at Karunya Institute of Technology and Sciences, where I also coordinate the Internal Quality Assurance Cell and lead Institution Relations. The research runs from masonry strengthening to construction 3D printing. None of it is separable from the rest.

The through line

From Building Structures to Building Lives

Engineering taught me how structures stand, how forces act and how systems can be strengthened.

Over time I began to see another kind of structure that matters even more. People. Confidence. Institutions. Communities. The next generation.

That realisation has shaped the journey from structural engineering and research into education, leadership, mentorship and service. The engineering has not been left behind — it is the method I still think with.

Research Trajectory

Twenty years, one question, several forms.

How do you make a structure behave better using what is actually available to build with? The answer has changed as the tools have.

  1. 2006–2011

    Foundation

    Civil engineering, then structural engineering. Roof truss configuration as a first research question.

  2. 2011–2018

    Masonry and seismic behaviour

    Doctoral work on confining masonry infill in RC frames with skin reinforcement — experimental, analytical and numerical.

  3. 2018–2022

    From strengthening to invention

    Latex-modified mortar, equivalent strut width, composite frames and damping systems; the GI-netting patent granted in 2022.

  4. 2022–present

    Printing, data and automation

    Printable cementitious materials, print-head and multi-arm systems, scan-to-BIM, and deep learning for crack characterisation.

Featured Innovation

Research that became something buildable.

A granted patent, published applications and a funded prototype — each arising from a problem met in the laboratory.

Granted

GI Nettings for Partially Restrained RCC Beam–Column Joint

A reinforcement approach using characterised GI wire netting at RCC beam–column joints, developed to address reinforcement congestion and improve seismic performance.

Beam–column joints carry the highest concentration of reinforcement in a moment-resisting frame, and that congestion makes them difficult to detail and to cast well. This method introduces characterised galvanised-iron netting at the joint as a partial restraint, reducing the shear reinforcement needed in the confined region while maintaining joint performance under cyclic demand.

Published application

3D Printing Head for Reinforced Concrete Structural Elements

A print-head arrangement for depositing reinforced concrete structural elements, addressing the central difficulty of combining extrusion with reinforcement.

Conventional concrete 3D printing deposits material in layers but leaves reinforcement as a separate, largely manual operation. This application covers a print head intended to bring the two together at the point of deposition for structural elements.

TriFusion 3D Automated Building Construction System

An automated building-construction system combining multiple print arms so that deposition and reinforcement placement can proceed together.

TriFusion is the system-level counterpart to the print-head work: rather than treating printing, reinforcement and finishing as sequential operations, it coordinates them across multiple arms. It is the invention behind the multi-arm printing project currently funded under the Karunya Short-Term Research Grant.
